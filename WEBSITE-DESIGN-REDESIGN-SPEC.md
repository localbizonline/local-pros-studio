# Website Design Sales Page — Complete Redesign Spec

Status: design and sales-flow specification only. No live page or production code has been changed.

Selected visual direction: Option 1's dark, cinematic, proof-led page, with Option 3's full "Cheap Website vs Local Pros" comparison inserted after the client portfolio proof and before the pricing close.

## TL;DR

Replace the current tidy brochure page with a dark, proof-heavy sales page that feels like the Home, Reviews, and Social Posting pages.

The new page should sell one practical outcome: **a local business website that makes the customer trust you and contact you before they contact the next contractor.**

It should not lead with features, process, or a gallery. It should lead with the commercial pain, show what the customer decides in the first few seconds, prove Local Pros can solve it with real client websites, remove the fear of a drawn-out website project, and then present the R9,900 offer.

## Why The Existing Page Feels Weak

The current page is neat, but it does not create enough desire or urgency.

- The headline, "Professional Websites That Get Results," could belong to any web company.
- It starts explaining the service before making the visitor feel the cost of a weak website.
- The four benefit icons are generic and visually quiet.
- The portfolio is treated as a gallery instead of sales proof.
- The process and price appear before enough trust and emotional momentum have been built.
- The white brochure styling does not match the confident dark sales-letter system used by the strongest pages.
- The long terms section interrupts the buying journey. Terms should remain available, but collapsed after the main close.
- There is no memorable transformation: old/weak website versus credible, mobile-ready, quote-generating business.

## Design Language To Carry Across

Use the existing successful pages as the design system:

- Near-black and charcoal section backgrounds, alternating to create rhythm.
- Amber/yellow for section labels, important words, trust markers, and main CTA buttons.
- White heavy headings with Bricolage Grotesque; Source Sans 3 for body copy.
- Short paragraphs, deliberate line breaks, blunt conversational copy, and generous spacing.
- Large proof images, not rows of small decorative cards.
- Rounded images and proof panels with restrained borders and shadows.
- Full-width yellow CTA buttons on mobile; repeated WhatsApp CTA with the same wording.
- Real South African local-business imagery and real client websites.
- Mobile-first composition. The phone version of each client site is as important as desktop.

### 2. Problem — The customer checks you before calling

Section label: `THE REAL PROBLEM`

Headline:

> You can do brilliant work and still lose the job to the business that looks better online.

Copy rhythm:

> A customer gets your name.  
> They Google you.  
> They open your website on their phone.  
> And before you ever speak to them, they make a decision.

Pain list:

- The website looks old or broken on mobile.
- They cannot quickly see what you do or where you work.
- There is no proof that real customers trust you.
- The phone number or WhatsApp button is hard to find.
- Your competitor looks established, active, and safer.

Pull quote:

> The best contractor does not always win. The contractor who looks easiest to trust often wins first.

Visual: a phone showing a cramped, dated page beside a polished Local Pros client page. The dated example must be generic and not imply that a named client had a poor site.

### 3. The buying moment — Show what happens in 10 seconds

Section label: `WHAT YOUR CUSTOMER NEEDS TO SEE`

Headline:

> In ten seconds, your website must answer five questions.

Use a large annotated client-site visual with five simple callouts:

1. Are you the right business for this job?
2. Do you work in my area?
3. Can I see proof of your work?
4. Do other customers trust you?
5. How do I call or WhatsApp you now?

Close:

> If those answers are obvious, the customer moves closer to contacting you. If they have to hunt, they leave.

### 7. How it works — Make it feel easy

### Asset 3 — Responsive proof close-up

Canvas: 1600 × 1100.

Prompt direction:

> Create a clean, front-facing responsive website presentation from the supplied desktop and mobile screenshots. Large laptop and phone aligned on one shared dark charcoal surface, accurate screenshot perspective, subtle amber underlight, generous negative space. Do not redesign the website shown inside the devices. No labels or decorative UI elements.

### Screenshot capture rules

- Capture current desktop at 1440 px wide and mobile at 390 px wide.
- Capture the hero plus enough of the next section to show page depth.
- Dismiss cookie banners and chat bubbles only when they cover the site.
- Do not alter client logos, wording, phone numbers, or visual identity inside a screenshot.
- Save a raw screenshot and the final generated mockup separately.
- Confirm the live screenshot is current before publishing it as portfolio proof.

## Desktop Layout Rules

- Content width: 1,080–1,200 px.
- Sales-letter copy width: 720–820 px.
- Hero heading: 64–76 px.
- Section heading: 42–54 px.
- Body: 18–21 px for narrative sections.
- Section spacing: 96–120 px.
- Use one dominant image per section.
- Keep CTA width between 360 and 520 px; do not make every section a card grid.

## Mobile Layout Rules

- 24 px side padding.
- Hero heading: 40–46 px, with no more than five short lines.
- Body: 18 px.
- CTA fills the content width.
- Proof images remain readable; do not shrink three devices into one tiny strip.
- Stack case studies as image, outcome label, explanation, link.
- Sticky bottom CTA remains available but must not cover FAQ or terms controls.

## Build Acceptance Checklist

- The first screen states a specific business outcome, not a generic web-design promise.
- Real website proof appears in the hero and again before pricing.
- The page matches the dark/amber Home, Reviews, and Social Posting system.
- Every major section moves the visitor through pain, understanding, proof, relief, or action.
- There is one repeated primary CTA phrase and one WhatsApp destination.
- Desktop and mobile screenshots of at least three current client sites are used.
- No invented lead, ranking, or revenue guarantees.
- Pricing and terms match the current approved offer.
- The terms remain accessible but do not interrupt the sales close.
- The page is visually checked at 390 px, 768 px, and 1440 px widths.
- CTA links, portfolio links, FAQ controls, and mobile sticky CTA are tested.

## Recommended Direction

Use the dark, cinematic, proof-first direction as the primary concept. It is the closest fit to the pages already working well, makes the client websites look more premium, and gives the sales copy enough contrast and drama without introducing a new brand system.
